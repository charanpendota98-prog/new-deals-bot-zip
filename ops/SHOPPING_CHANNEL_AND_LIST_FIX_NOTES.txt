Three rejection risks were still open. All three are now enforced in code, so

1. LINK-LEVEL DISCLOSURE  (the biggest one)
-------------------------------------------
Amazon/FTC require a clear disclosure NEXT TO the link on EVERY post. The
channel bio alone is NOT enough - Amazon's own help page says the link-level
disclosure must be "clear" and "conspicuous", and names "(paid link)", "#ad",
"#CommissionsEarned" as acceptable. Missing it is one of the most common
rejection/warning reasons.

    SHOPPING_DISCLOSURE="#ad (paid link)"    (default)

add_link_disclosure() appends it to every review-channel post, at delivery,
AFTER every cleaning pass - so no later pass can strip it - and never twice.

    Boat Airdopes 141 at 899
    https://www.amazon.in/dp/B0X

    #ad (paid link)

2. AMAZON ONLY
--------------
The channel is submitted for the AMAZON programme, so a Flipkart/Myntra link on
it is off-programme noise to a reviewer.

    SHOPPING_AMAZON_ONLY=true   (default)

Only a post whose links are ALL Amazon reaches this channel. A mixed post
(Amazon + Flipkart) is excluded too - otherwise the off-programme link rides
along. Everything else keeps going to the other channels exactly as today.
Enforced twice: at routing AND at delivery.

3. A DAILY CAP
--------------
20-30 curated posts/day reads like a shop. 200 reads like spam and is itself a
rejection risk.

    SHOPPING_DAILY_CAP=30       (default; 0 = unlimited)

(an in-memory counter would reset to zero every deploy and quietly blow the
cap). Old rows are pruned after 3 days.

every other channel still receives it.

----------------------------
Default is 30/day. For a tighter 20:

The channel takes the deals as they come; if your Amazon flow exceeds the cap,
the surplus simply goes to the other channels.

No loot/urgency wording, no "buy max quantity", no unverifiable claims, no
